# Forwardable Enterprise AI Decks

Use this reference when a founder needs a general PDF deck that prospects, partners, or internal champions can forward without the founder present.

## Distinguish the artifact from a live talk

A live presentation can depend on narration, progressive reveals, room interaction, and sparse projected copy. A forwardable PDF must carry its own context, definitions, evidence, boundaries, and next step. Do not force one artifact to perform both jobs unchanged.

Before visual production, deliver a text-only page breakdown for approval. Include exact headlines, supporting copy, proof requirements, claim boundaries, and appendices. Do not generate the PDF until the user approves the message architecture.

## Ground requirements in buyer evidence

1. Inspect the live mailbox and complete buyer-authored threads when the user names email as the source.
2. Search call transcripts for direct requests for decks, case studies, examples, proof, capabilities, use cases, forwardable material, pricing, support, ownership, and security.
3. Separate buyer requests from provider offers to send something, internal debriefs, and unrelated uses of `deck`, `send`, or `share`.
4. Preserve short exact buyer wording and the information gap it reveals; minimize unrelated private content.
5. Treat a deck request as a collateral need, not proof of buying intent.
6. Use live websites as current positioning sources, but classify public performance, customer, integration, and security copy as vendor claims unless independently evidenced.

## Questions the deck must answer unaided

A reader unfamiliar with the company should be able to explain:

1. What is the company in one sentence?
2. Is the offer software, service, or both?
3. What can the system actually do?
4. Where could my organization use it?
5. How does one workflow operate end to end?
6. What has genuinely been built or accepted?
7. What remains human-controlled?
8. How does implementation begin and progress?
9. Why not use a general AI tool, native module, specialist product, agency, or internal team?
10. What is the next step, what does it produce, and who is a fit?

If any answer requires founder narration, revise the page.

## Recommended message progression

Use progressive specificity:

1. plain-language promise;
2. buyer's execution problem;
3. concrete capability reveal;
4. where the capability applies;
5. one full trigger-to-record workflow;
6. proof with status and constraints;
7. implementation and operating model;
8. human authority and security controls;
9. fit, alternatives, and next step.

For enterprise-agent decks, a strong repeated workflow frame is:

`trigger → approved context → defined agent work → exception path → human decision → system update → verification and record`

For each use case show:

`problem → agent action → human control → observable value signal`

Prefer 10-14 core pages plus modular appendices over one large deck that mixes sales, fundraising, procurement, architecture, and live-talk material.

## Commercial without generic pitching

Let the artifact demonstrate expertise through concrete workflows, implementation choices, controls, and honest no-build alternatives. Avoid repeated provider claims and capability walls. The reader should learn something useful even if they do not buy.

Use an execution-gap antagonist, not fear of AI or employee replacement: the wrong workflow, no owner, broken source data, excessive authority, happy-path demos, weak adoption, or no production operating model.

End with one substantive action such as selecting a workflow for assessment. State what that step evaluates or produces. Avoid a vague `learn more` or `book a demo` CTA unless the user asks.

## Proof gate

Do not substitute a logo wall for proof. Each case study should state:

- operating context;
- exact workflow and trigger;
- systems/data involved;
- what the agent or system does;
- what remains human-owned;
- implementation and acceptance stage;
- output;
- measured result or `measurement pending`;
- difficult exception/failure mode;
- evidence and publicity permission.

Keep `contracted`, `proof-stage`, `deployed`, `accepted`, and `measured` distinct. Public website publication does not automatically prove event/PDF reuse permission. When approval is absent, use a verified internal demonstration or anonymized workflow pattern with an accurate label.

## Buyer versus investor variants

Do not let a general buyer deck imply a fundraise. Keep the operating-company narrative as the core. If the recipient is an investor and a raise is active, add a separately approved appendix covering business model, traction with cutoff date, market, defensibility, team, raise amount, use of funds, milestones, and explicit ask. If no raise is active, state that plainly.

Likewise, put detailed pricing, architecture, integrations, security/procurement, RACI, and expanded use cases in modular appendices rather than burdening the core.

## Inspiration-site discipline

Borrow structure, not protected expression or halo. It is acceptable to adapt patterns such as progressive specificity, a common discovery phase, explicit delivery branches, evidence cards, and a repeated CTA. Do not copy wording, composition, visual system, partner/customer logos, acquisition credibility, or another vendor's outcome claims.

## Claim-safe value language

Avoid universal time-saved or ROI claims. Show how value will be evaluated:

- cycle time and queue age;
- search and coordination burden;
- missing information and rework;
- exception detection;
- reviewer corrections and approval burden;
- adoption and reliability;
- operational outcome tied to the workflow.

State that targets and outcomes are workflow-specific.

## Acceptance test before PDF generation

- [ ] Text-only page architecture approved before design.
- [ ] Core deck is self-contained and forwardable.
- [ ] Product/service relationship is explicit.
- [ ] Concrete capabilities appear before deep architecture.
- [ ] At least one end-to-end workflow includes exception, approval, write-back, and verification.
- [ ] Proof status and publicity permission are verified.
- [ ] Buyer and investor narratives are not mixed.
- [ ] Every client, outcome, integration, and security claim has evidence classification.
- [ ] CTA states the next action and what it produces.
- [ ] Appendices are modular by recipient type.
