# From funnel audit to launch plan and source-to-cash scoreboard

Part 1 covers launch readiness and the KPI scoreboard. Part 2 adds account-based marketing (ABM) integration when a named-account campaign already exists.

## Part 1. Campaign readiness and source-to-cash

Use this reference when a user asks for a live-funnel audit plus the fastest launch sequence and KPI scoreboard.

### Reconcile four evidence states

Inventory every important asset and classify it separately as:

1. **observed live**: inspected directly now;
2. **reported live**: shown in a dated screenshot/addendum but not authenticated in the current audit;
3. **approved candidate**: locally QA-passed and eligible for a future action;
4. **plan/draft/rejected/blocked**: not launch-ready.

Do not let a clean file, high match-rate screenshot, polished creative, or detailed launch plan silently become evidence of a live campaign. Preserve explicit rejection history. A rejected creative remains blocked even when its technical QA passed.

### Audit message continuity across boundaries

Compare these surfaces side by side:

`traffic promise → landing headline → CTA → scheduler/event description → duration → form fields → confirmation → sales stage → first paid decision`

Flag contradictions such as:

- landing page promises 30 minutes while the scheduler books 40;
- corporate language promises supervised, approval-gated operation while the event says "autopilot";
- an enterprise CTA opens a generic intro event;
- the form requires phone but omits company, role, workflow, urgency, systems, or owner;
- an owned page carries UTMs but the embedded scheduler URL drops them;
- current pages use one category while legacy case-study/footer templates use another.

Inspect the scheduler itself without confirming a booking. Select a public slot only far enough to reveal duration, description, required fields, source questions, privacy copy, and attendee options.

### Inspect consent before making a choice

In a fresh browser state, record whether analytics/ad/session-replay requests occur while the consent banner is still awaiting a decision. Test three states when authorized:

1. no choice;
2. decline;
3. accept.

Report observed requests, not legal conclusions. Treat pre-choice collection as a privacy-review blocker until the approved consent model and implementation are verified.

### Reconcile public proof with the claims ledger

A live case-study or logo page is not automatically safe for reuse in paid media, outbound, decks, or campaign landing pages. For each claim, reconcile:

- exact legal/operating entity;
- relationship and delivery stage;
- baseline, period, metric owner, and attribution limit;
- production acceptance evidence;
- permission for name, logo, testimonial, outcome, and paid-media use;
- whether the page is a legacy template with outdated category or CTA language.

Classify each item as `cleared`, `revise`, or `blocked from campaign reuse`.

### Prefer one conversion bridge over a full rebuild

When the corporate site is credible but broad, launch the smallest message-matched bridge page rather than rebuilding the whole site. The page should:

- name one buyer decision and one first-workflow outcome;
- explain who qualifies and what does not fit;
- show the method and human authority boundary;
- use only permission-cleared proof;
- collect qualification fields that change routing;
- persist source IDs and UTMs;
- route to direct calendar, human review/readiness, existing-owner, or no-fit paths.

Do not intentionally send campaign or event traffic until one authorized test proves the same source ID across landing, application, booking, CRM read-back, confirmation, opportunity, and eventually invoice/payment records.

### Build a source-to-cash scoreboard

Use original source as the acquisition credit and keep influenced sources separate. Required stages:

`qualified landing session → application → booking → held call → accepted qualified held → artifact/operator session → paid-evidence quote → signed → cash collected → production acceptance → expansion`

For each source report raw denominators, integer outcomes, and rejection reasons. Keep these states separate:

- platform impression/reach;
- tagged session;
- server-confirmed application;
- booking;
- held meeting;
- accepted qualified meeting;
- opportunity stage;
- quote/proposal;
- signature;
- cleared cash;
- production acceptance.

A proposal is not revenue, a signature is not cash, and payment is not production acceptance.

### Fast launch sequence

Default sequence:

1. freeze one message and one first commercial decision;
2. align page, scheduler, duration, form, privacy, and qualification;
3. verify source persistence and CRM read-back end to end;
4. repair event/QR/deep-link routes and test on physical phones;
5. reconcile claims, logos, suppressions, and audience state;
6. launch small warm and organic motions first;
7. keep audience uploads, paid builds, activation, and spend behind separate approvals;
8. review source-to-cash denominators after 7 and 14 days;
9. scale, hold, revise, or stop each source independently.

### Reporting shape

Lead with:

1. decisive launch/hold recommendation;
2. top cross-boundary contradictions;
3. asset-state matrix;
4. fastest viable funnel;
5. source-to-cash KPI scoreboard;
6. 7- or 14-day sequence with owners and acceptance evidence;
7. limitations and source ledger.

Keep the chat response short. Put the full evidence and operating detail in a durable report.

## Part 2. ABM integration

Use this reference when the task is not only to critique a landing page, but to reconcile a live funnel with existing account-based marketing strategy, creative, roster, and operating assets.

### Reuse-first evidence inventory

Before recommending a campaign, locate and reconcile:

- latest creative renders and editable sources;
- prior creative audit and explicit approval/rejection history;
- canonical account/contact uploads and QA summaries;
- role/current-title verification and committee-completeness evidence;
- CRM relationship-clearance and suppression ledgers;
- category, offer, claims, proof, pricing, and founder-led outreach strategy;
- campaign matrices, belief-gap definitions, ownership, waves, and work queues.

Do not treat a file labeled "approved" as proof that its current titles, claims, logos, CTA, or audience are launch-approved. Separate approval of a visual direction, exact asset, and final launch.

### Integrated audit sequence

1. Map the real ad-to-revenue path: `creative → landing page → CTA → qualifier/form → scheduler → confirmation → routing → qualification → commercial event`.
2. Compare creative promise, landing-page message, CTA wording, destination offer, price, and scheduler meeting type side by side.
3. Load the live landing URL with a representative UTM query and inspect whether every CTA preserves source fields across domains.
4. Distinguish tags present, requests observed, and conversions proven. A page view or pixel request does not prove booked-call conversion, deduplication, consent correctness, hidden-field capture, or CRM attribution.
5. Capture desktop and mobile evidence. If the standard browser cannot set a viewport, use an available headless browser to save fixed-size screenshots, then inspect them visually.
6. Reconcile account segmentation with actual data coverage. Do not propose asset-class or role variants when those fields are mostly blank, stale, or inferred.
7. Produce an explicit `launch now / revise / blocked` matrix for logos, claims, offer/CTA, landing page, form/tracking, titles/committee, suppressions, audience forecast, and budget.
8. Turn recommendations into an operating plan: account belief gap, committee role, ad layer, offer/CTA, founder/operator daily action, human approval gate, conversion event, UTM, KPI, suppression rule, budget envelope, and scale/kill threshold.

### Belief-gap and committee design

Use the account, not the lead or impression, as the unit of orchestration. Common gaps are problem, value, trust, implementation, vendor, and timing. Assign a gap only when evidence supports it; unknown is not evidence.

At minimum map:

- economic buyer: consequence, priority, budget, accountability;
- workflow owner: handoffs, exceptions, baseline, adoption;
- technical/security approver: systems, access, permissions, evaluation, rollback;
- connector: routing only, never forced advocacy.

Do not send identical messaging to the committee. Paid exposure is air cover, not proof that a named person saw an ad.

### Approval and suppression discipline

Keep these approvals separate and timestamped:

- account activation and suppression;
- current role/committee;
- exact creative and claims;
- audience/settings/tracking/budget;
- each human message, comment, DM, or email;
- opportunity creation and commercial proposal;
- final launch.

A point-in-time CRM clearance is not permanent. Recheck CRM, inbox/history, opt-outs, customers, opportunities, partners, employees, wrong affiliates, holdouts, and relationship owners before upload and before each direct action.

### Budget and decision rules

When no live ad-platform forecast is available, label budget figures as conditional test envelopes rather than approved spend. Require the live forecast before activation. Start with few cells so a small ABM audience is not fragmented. Scale only on account/role quality and stage movement, not impressions or CTR alone. Pause immediately for unsafe claims, wrong/suppressed delivery, broken destination/tracking, UTM loss, unreviewed audience expansion, complaints, or extreme spend concentration.

### Reporting pattern

Lead with:

1. the decisive launch/hold recommendation;
2. top blockers in priority order;
3. the first seven-day sequence;
4. durable report path.

The full report should include observed evidence versus inference, limitations, source paths/URLs, and acceptance tests for every blocker.
